Finding 01

A quarter of these questions are reruns

The 503 Domain 1 and 2 questions contain only 435 distinct stems. Sixty-eight of them appear on more than one exam, accounting for 135 questions — 27% of the pool. The exams are not independent samples; they share a core that keeps resurfacing.

That changes what studying means. A question you have already seen twice in this corpus is more likely to appear again than any question you have not seen. These are the ones that repeat most:

Finding 02

The letters move, the answers don't

When a question reappears, the option order is often reshuffled. The Trusted Advisor question keys to A on exam 9 and B on exam 17 — identical question, identical answer text, different letter.

Anything you remember as "that one's C" is worthless and actively misleading. Memorize the answer text. When you review a missed question, write down the service or phrase, never the letter.

Finding 03

Some services are almost always the decoy

Counting every appearance of a service across all 503 Domain 1 and 2 questions, and splitting by whether it was the correct answer or a wrong option, produces a strikingly uneven picture.

Amazon Inspector appears 30 times and is right 3 of them. It is this corpus's favourite wrong answer, and you have picked it once already. Amazon Connect — a call-centre service with no business in either domain — appears 14 times and is never correct. Certificate Manager, Secrets Manager and Systems Manager are likewise pure noise inside these two domains.

At the other end, AWS Artifact is right 72% of the times it appears. If Artifact is on the list and the question mentions compliance, reports or agreements, it is almost certainly the answer.

This is a prior, not a rule

These rates describe one corpus of 23 practice exams, not the real exam. Systems Manager and Secrets Manager are genuinely correct answers in Domain 3 contexts and on the real test. Use this to break a tie between two options you are already unsure about — never to overrule something you actually know.

Finding 04

Shared responsibility is the single most-tested idea

Sixty-six questions in the corpus turn on the shared responsibility model — about one in every eight Domain 1 and 2 questions. No other concept comes close. It is also where you are weakest: 3 of 10 across your two exams.

Every one of those 66 questions is answered by sorting a task into one of three buckets. Here is what the corpus actually puts in each:

AWS ALONE — security of the cloud

  • Edge location management
  • Securing the EC2 hypervisor
  • Updating EC2 host firmware
  • Hardware maintenance
  • Physical and environmental controls
  • Configuring infrastructure devices
  • Securing Regions and edge locations
  • Patching managed database software

CUSTOMER ALONE — security in the cloud

  • Patching EC2 instances / guest OS
  • Encrypting data client-side
  • Data encryption at rest
  • Configuring network ACLs
  • Configuring security groups
  • Ensuring EBS volumes are backed up
  • Training your users on AWS security
  • Granting access to individuals and services

SHARED — only these three

  • Patch management
  • Configuration management
  • Awareness and training

The pattern in the two lists is one rule: anything you can configure or see is yours; anything below the hypervisor is AWS's. Note where the corpus puts the traps — "operating system maintenance" is AWS's when the service is managed (RDS) and yours when it is EC2, so the service named in the stem decides the answer, not the task.

The three shared controls are worth memorising verbatim

Patch management, configuration management, awareness and training. Nothing else is shared. Every "which is a shared control" question in this corpus answers with one of those three, and you have missed this question type twice — once naming data center auditing, once calling configuration management solely the customer's.

The Config / CloudTrail split is the one to internalise, because both stems use the word audit and the corpus tests them 18 times between them. Config answers "what did this resource look like, and how did it change". CloudTrail answers "who called which API, and when". The noun after "audit" decides it: a resource means Config, an action or identity means CloudTrail.

Finding 06

Hybrid connectivity is Domain 1's most-answered question

The two most frequent correct answers in all of Domain 1 are not concepts at all: AWS Direct Connect (9 times) and AWS Storage Gateway (8 times). They come almost entirely from one repeating question family — "which services extend an on-premises architecture to AWS" and its variants.

The answer set is small and stable: Direct Connect for a dedicated private line, AWS VPN (3 times) for an encrypted tunnel over the internet, Storage Gateway for extending on-premises storage into AWS. CloudFront, Route 53, Amazon Connect and EBS recur as the wrong options in the same question — you have picked Route 53 and CloudFront here before.

After those, Domain 1's recurring answers are exactly the vocabulary you would expect: Availability Zones (6), elasticity / implement elasticity (10 combined), loose coupling (4), agility (3), and the TCO Calculator (6) for before-you-migrate cost comparisons.

Finding 07

Three answer keys in this corpus are wrong

Comparing repeated questions by answer text rather than letter surfaced three genuine disagreements. One matters:

Exam 14, "Which task is AWS responsible for?"

Exam 14 keys this to "Encrypting data in transit". That is wrong — configuring encryption in transit is the customer's responsibility. The identical question on exam 9 offers the same options and correctly keys "Updating Amazon EC2 host firmware". If you take exam 14, that key will teach you the exact misconception you are already carrying.

The other two are trivial wording differences ("Awareness" vs "Awareness and training"). But the lesson generalises: a community answer key is not authoritative. When a key contradicts the shared responsibility rule above, the key is wrong.

Finding 08

Where your marks are actually going

Across exams 9 and 17 you scored 73% on single-answer questions and 52% on Choose TWO. On all eleven multi-response questions you missed, you had exactly one of the two answers right. Not one where you were lost.

That is the largest single recoverable block of marks available to you, and it needs no new knowledge. The decoy table above explains why the technique works: your second pick tends to land on a high-frequency decoy — Inspector, CodeDeploy, EC2-as-edge-service, Data Pipeline. Tested as a standalone true-or-false statement, each of those dies immediately.

The procedure

Read all five options. For each one, ask "is this statement true on its own?" and mark it true or false without reference to the others. Then select the two you marked true. Never ask "which two go together" — that question is what makes a plausible-sounding decoy attractive.

Your study order

  • The ten repeated questions in Finding 01. Highest density of marks per minute in this entire corpus, and you have already missed two of them twice.
  • The three-bucket sort in Finding 04. One in eight questions in these domains, and your weakest area at 3/10. Memorise the three shared controls verbatim.
  • The Choose TWO procedure in Finding 08. Method, not knowledge. Worth about five marks per exam on your current numbers.
  • Config versus CloudTrail, tested 18 times between them. Resource state versus API caller.
  • Treat Inspector, Cognito, GuardDuty, Certificate Manager, Secrets Manager, Systems Manager and Amazon Connect as suspects when they appear in a Domain 1 or 2 question. Between them they appear 106 times in this pool and are correct 5 times.
