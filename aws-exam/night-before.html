1. The Choose TWO habit

30 min

Across every practice exam, on almost every Choose TWO you missed, you had exactly one of the two answers right. Not one where you were lost. You find the obvious answer, then pick a partner that sounds like it belongs with it rather than testing it on its own.

The fix is mechanical, and it is the only thing tonight you should practise until it is automatic:

  1. Cover the other options. Read option A alone.
  2. Ask one question: "is this statement true, by itself?" Not "does it fit with the others". Mark it T or F.
  3. Repeat for B, C, D, E without looking back at what you marked.
  4. Select the two you marked T. If you marked three, re-read them for an absolute ("all", "never", "eliminates") — that one is false.
  5. Count your selections before moving on. One selection scores zero. So does three.

Twice now you have submitted a single letter for a Choose TWO question. On the real exam that is an automatic zero on a question you may well have half-known. Before you leave any multi-response question, count the boxes.

How to actually sit the exam

65 questions, 90 minutes. That is 83 seconds each, and most questions should take half that. Your problem will not be time; it will be rushing the ones that need care.

The protocol

  1. Read the last sentence of the question first, then the options, then go back for the scenario only if you still need it. Most of the scenario is decoration.
  2. Name the answer before you look at the options. If you can, find it and move on in ten seconds. If you cannot, you are guessing — flag it and go.
  3. Never spend more than 90 seconds on a question. Flag, pick your best guess anyway, move on. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so never leave one blank.
  4. On every Choose TWO, run the T/F pass and count your selections. This is the one thing worth slowing down for.
  5. Eliminate absolutes. "All", "never", "eliminates", "guarantees", "no need to" — almost always wrong. The one exception: explicit deny always wins in IAM.
  6. Prefer the managed AWS service over any option describing manual work or custom tooling. If AWS ships a switch for the requirement, that is the answer.
  7. Read the service name in the scenario before answering anything about patching, responsibility or configuration. EC2 and RDS give opposite answers to the same question.
  8. First pass: answer everything you know, flag the rest. Aim to finish the first pass in 50 minutes. Then spend the remaining time on flagged questions only.
  9. Do not change an answer on the second pass unless you can name the specific reason the new one is right. Second-guessing costs more than it gains.
